# card-links: help

You are working out how the parts of a piece connect: a plot, an argument, notes toward an essay. card-links shows your document as a canvas of cards that you can drag anywhere, and lets you draw labelled links between them.

## What it gives you

- **The canvas** (replaces the page in the pane): one numbered card per block, in document order. You type straight in a card and it edits the document. Horizontal rules get no card.
- **Drag handle** (the top strip of each card, showing its number): drag it to move the card. Its place is saved when you let go.
- **Link button** (the small chain icon on a card's top strip): drag from it and release on another card to link them. A banner says what is happening and a dashed line follows the pointer. **Esc**, or releasing on empty canvas, makes nothing.
- **Link label** (in the middle of a link, "label" until you name it): double-click to edit it, **Enter** or clicking away saves, **Esc** cancels. Click it once to select the link, then click more cards to add them as targets.
- **Delete link** (the small button on a label, or **Delete** or **Backspace** with the link selected): removes the link.
- **New card** (button at the top of the canvas, and in the pane's tab menu): adds an empty block at the end, as a new card.
- **Undo canvas edit** and **Redo canvas edit** (tab menu and palette): step back or forward through moves, link changes and typing.

## Walkthrough

1. Open a document with at least five paragraphs. Turn on card-links for the pane from its tab menu. Each paragraph becomes a numbered card.
2. Drag a card by its top strip to a new spot. The others stay where they are.
3. Drag from a card's link button and release on another card. A curve appears with a "label" placeholder.
4. Double-click the label, type "causes" and press **Enter**.
5. Click the label to select the link, then click a third card to add it as a second target.
6. With the link selected, press **Delete** to remove it. Choose **Undo canvas edit** from the tab menu to bring it back.
7. Click **New card** and type in it to add a new part to the document.
8. Turn the module off to get the plain editor back, then on again: your positions and links are still there.

## How the pieces fit

Cards are the document's blocks, so editing a card edits the document, and other panes show the changes live. Moving a card never changes the order of the text; it only changes where the card sits, and links record the connections instead. Positions and links are saved with the file and show up for anyone who opens it.

## Tips

- Enter does not split a card; one card is one block.
- Deleting a card removes its links; deleting a target only removes that target, and the link goes once none are left.
- Cards from other modules' block types get a grid slot but cannot be moved or linked.
- Everything, including typing, undoes in order with **Cmd-Z**.
