# deictic-highlighter: description

Colours words that point to the speaker, place or moment of a text, in five groups: personal words like I and you, places like here, times like now, connectives like however, and forms of address like sir. The colours are display only and update as you type.

For a writer, editor or student of language who wants to see how much of a text depends on its speaker, place and moment.

**Kind:** extension. It only colours text in an ordinary editing pane.

- **Five categories, five colours:** personal (pronouns: I, you, we, they, my, myself, it, one, ...), spatial (here, there, up, down, left, right, above, below, near, far, inside, ahead, ...), temporal (now, then, today, yesterday, soon, later, before, after, already, still, ...), discourse (thus, however, moreover, finally, hereby, ...) and social (sir, madam, doctor, Mr, Mrs, lord, ...). The word lists are fixed; the module doesn't learn. The first draft of the lists is the previous version's.
- **A legend.** The writer can open a small legend from the toolbar that names each colour and its category, so they can tell which colour means what. There are no per-category switches.
- **Whole words only,** case-insensitively: "Here" and "here" match, "where" and "hereby" don't match "here" (hereby is discourse). A word next to punctuation matches ("here,", "(now)").
- **Contractions and apostrophes.** "it's", "I'm", "we're" and "they'd" colour as personal, whole word, and "ma'am" as social; straight and curly apostrophes alike.
- **A word split by formatting** (bold in the middle of "today") is still one word and coloured whole.
- **One colour per word.** A word in more than one list takes the first of personal, social, discourse, temporal, spatial.
- **Live, everywhere.** Colours follow editing, in paragraphs, headings, lists and quotes. Text that other modules keep inside the text (notes, alternatives) is left alone.
- **Display only.** Nothing about the document changes; colours are not saved, copied or exported, and undo is unaffected. Colours work in light and dark themes and sit beside other highlighting modules without hiding their marks (where two colour the same word, either may show).
- **Every pane** the module is placed on shows the same colours; turning the module off removes them.
