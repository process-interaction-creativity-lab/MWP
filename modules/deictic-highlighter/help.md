# deictic-highlighter: help

You are reading or editing a text and want to see how much of it depends on who is speaking, where and when. This module colours the words that point at the speaker, place or moment, as you type.

## What it gives you

- **Personal** (colour in the text): words like I, you, we, they, my, myself, it.
- **Spatial**: words like here, there, up, down, left, above, inside.
- **Temporal**: words like now, then, today, yesterday, soon, before.
- **Discourse**: connectives like thus, however, moreover, finally, hereby.
- **Social**: forms of address like sir, madam, doctor, Mr, Mrs.
- **Deictic legend** (toolbar button; palette: **Show deictic word legend**): opens a small key that shows each colour beside its category name.

## Walkthrough

1. Turn the module on in the pane's tab menu.
2. Type or paste: "I think you should go there now, sir. However, the doctor left yesterday. Here is my idea."
3. Look at the colours: "I", "you" and "my" share one; "there" and "Here" another; "now" and "yesterday" another; "However" another; "sir" and "doctor" another.
4. Press **Deictic legend** in the toolbar to check which colour means what.
5. Keep typing. Colours follow your edits, in paragraphs, headings, lists and quotes.
6. Try "where" and "overhead": they stay plain, because only whole words match. "Hereby" is coloured as discourse.

## How the pieces fit

Every word is checked against five fixed word lists, matched whole and without regard to capitals. A word in more than one list takes the first of personal, social, discourse, temporal, spatial. Contractions such as "it's" and "I'm" colour whole as personal, and "ma'am" as social. A word split by formatting, such as a bold stretch in the middle of "today", is still coloured whole.

## Tips

- The colours are display only: they aren't saved, copied or exported, and undo is not affected.
- The word lists are fixed; the module doesn't learn new words, and there are no per-category switches.
- Every pane that has the module shows the same colours; turning it off removes them.
