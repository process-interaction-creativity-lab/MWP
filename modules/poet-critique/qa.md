# QA: poet-critique

About five minutes. Needs AI turned on for the module in the host's AI settings with a working key. Have a document with a short poem of at least four lines.

1. With AI off for the module (or no key), find the critique command in the palette. Confirm it is unavailable and says AI is needed, and nothing errors. Turn AI on.
2. With nothing highlighted, look at the critique button and the palette command. Confirm both are greyed or do nothing, with a hint to highlight text first.
3. Highlight two lines, choose a poet, and ask from the toolbar button (then try the bubble over the selection). Open the poet-critique side tab if it is closed. Confirm a portrait and speech bubble appear there, beside the text, the quoted excerpt is shortened on one line, and a "thinking" state with running seconds shows. Confirm you cannot start a second request while one is pending.
4. When the reply arrives, confirm it is in the poet's voice, points at specific lines, keeps paragraph breaks, and scrolls if long. Edit the poem while it shows and confirm the bubble and its excerpt do not change.
5. Change the poet while the reply is showing; confirm the old reply keeps its old poet's face. Ask again for the new face. Pick a custom poet, type a name, ask twice, and confirm the portrait appears and stays the same.
6. Ask and dismiss the companion before the reply arrives; confirm nothing appears later. Ask again, turn AI off for the module mid-request, and confirm the request is abandoned and the control is unavailable.
7. Go offline (or use a bad key) and ask. Confirm a readable error appears in the bubble, not a blank or a stuck "thinking".
8. Open a second pane on the same document. Ask in one pane; click into the other and confirm the side tab shows that pane's (empty) companion, then click back and confirm the critique is still there. Close the side tab, ask again, reopen it and confirm the reply arrived. Turn the module off and on; confirm the side tab went and came back without the critique, and the poet choice is remembered.
