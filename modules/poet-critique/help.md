# poet-critique: help

You have a draft poem and want a second reaction, in a famous voice. Highlight some lines, ask, and a small pixel-art cartoon of a poet answers beside your text with feedback in that poet's voice. Nothing is added to your poem.

## What it gives you

- **Critique** (toolbar button, with a speech-quote icon; also the button in the selection bubble; palette: **Ask a poet for a critique**): asks for a critique of the highlighted lines. With nothing highlighted it is greyed, with a hint to highlight something first.
- **poet-critique** (side tab): the companion, beside your text. It shows the poet's portrait, the quoted excerpt, and a speech bubble with the reply.
- **Poet** (menu at the top of the tab): choose Emily Dickinson, Walt Whitman, Robert Frost, William Shakespeare, Langston Hughes, Sylvia Plath, Pablo Neruda or Maya Angelou, or **Custom...**, which shows a **Poet's name** box for any name you like.
- **Critique selection** (button in the tab): the same ask, from inside the companion.
- **Dismiss** (x in the tab): clears the companion, and abandons a reply still pending.
- **Thinking...** (in the bubble): shows the poet thinking with the seconds counting up. If the request fails, a readable message appears in the bubble instead.

## Walkthrough

1. Turn the module on in the pane's tab menu, with AI set up. Open the **poet-critique** side tab if it isn't already open.
2. Pick a poet from the **Poet** menu, or choose **Custom...** and type a name.
3. Highlight two or three lines of your poem.
4. Press **Critique** in the toolbar (or in the bubble over your selection). The excerpt appears in the tab with the poet's portrait and a "thinking" note.
5. Read the reply in the speech bubble; it scrolls if long. Keep editing your poem if you like; the excerpt and reply stay as they were.
6. Change the poet and ask again: you get a new face, and an old reply keeps the face of the poet who gave it.
7. Press **Dismiss** when you're done.

## How the pieces fit

The poet menu decides whose voice answers, and the highlighted lines decide what is critiqued. The same poet always gets the same portrait, including a custom one. Each pane has its own companion, so a critique stays with the pane you asked in. The poet you chose is remembered across reloads and documents; the critique itself is not saved with the file.

## Tips

- Needs AI: set a key and model in Settings, in the project tab's menu. Without it, asking is unavailable and says to turn AI on.
- Only one request can run at a time. Turning AI off, or turning the module off, abandons a pending one.
- If the tab is closed when you ask, the request still runs; open the tab to read the reply.
- Nothing is written to your text, so there is nothing to undo.
