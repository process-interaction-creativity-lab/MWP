# topic-sentence-highlighting: help

You are checking whether each paragraph states its point up front. This module highlights the first sentence of every paragraph and blurs the rest, so the document reads as a skeleton of topic sentences. It only changes how the text looks.

## What it gives you

- **Yellow highlight** (the text itself): the first sentence of each ordinary paragraph. A one-sentence paragraph is highlighted with nothing blurred; an empty paragraph shows nothing.
- **Blur** (the text itself): the rest of each paragraph. Headings, quotes, code blocks and lists are blurred as a whole, with no highlight inside them.
- **Hover to read**: point at a paragraph or block and its blur clears smoothly; move away and it returns.
- **Caret to read**: the paragraph or block you are typing in stays clear, so keyboard and touch work too.

There are no buttons: placing the module turns it on, and removing it turns it off.

## Walkthrough

1. Write a few paragraphs of three or four sentences, with a one-sentence paragraph, a heading and a list.
2. Place the module on the pane. The first sentence of each paragraph turns yellow and the rest blurs.
3. Read down the yellow sentences alone. Does each announce its paragraph's point? If one doesn't, rewrite it.
4. Hover a paragraph to read it in full. The highlight stays and other paragraphs stay blurred.
5. Click into a blurred paragraph or move into it with the arrow keys: it clears while your caret is in it. Edit the first sentence and the highlight follows as you type.
6. Remove the module from the pane to see the plain text again.

## How the pieces fit

The highlight tells you what the paragraph says first, and the blur keeps you from reading on and forgiving a weak opening. Hovering and the caret are your ways back into the full text. Nothing is saved, copied or searched with the effect, and undo is untouched; the text is identical with the module on or off. Two panes on the same document both show it, and a hover affects only its own pane.

## Tips

- A sentence ends at a full stop, question mark or exclamation mark followed by a space, so "Dr. Smith" ends the highlight early. This is accepted.
- Typing, deleting, pasting and undo all update the effect at once.
- Copying a highlighted paragraph elsewhere brings no highlight or blur with it.
