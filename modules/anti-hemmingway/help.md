# anti-hemmingway: help

You, or an editor, want to see how much of a draft is description. Anti-hemmingway greys out everything except adjectives and adverbs, so your modifiers stand out against a faded page.

## What it gives you

- **Grey text** (the text itself): every word that is not an adjective or adverb turns grey, along with the spaces and punctuation between words. Adjectives and adverbs keep the normal colour.
- **Live colouring** (the text itself): as you type, a word is greyed or kept as soon as you finish it.
- There are no buttons, toolbar entries or palette actions. Turning the module on or off is all there is.

## Walkthrough

1. Write or open a paragraph or two of your draft.
2. Turn the module on. Most of the page fades to grey; the words left dark are the adjectives and adverbs.
3. Scan a paragraph: a page that is mostly dark is heavy with description, a mostly grey one is spare.
4. Type a new sentence, for example "The quietly falling snow was very cold." and watch "quietly", "falling" and "cold" stay dark when you finish each word.
5. Turn the module off to get the normal colour back everywhere.

## How the pieces fit

This is the opposite of striking modifiers out: it only shows them to you. Your text, its marks, your selection and undo are never touched, and copied text carries no grey. With several panes on the document, every pane that has the module looks the same. Nothing is saved.

## Tips

- Words are judged one at a time, in English only, without sentence context, so expect some mistakes. Numbers and words under two letters are grey.
- Headings, lists and quotes are coloured too; code blocks keep their normal colour. Notes from other modules are left alone.
