# prosody: description

For poets writing in a set form. Choose a meter for the poem, or for a single stanza, and each line is marked with its stressed and unstressed syllables, with the places that break the meter flagged. It also finds your rhyme scheme and marks lines that depart from it.

**Kind:** extension. It marks up the ordinary editor's text and adds a picker and a report; the meters chosen are saved in the file. It is a checker, not a generator: the poet states the form and the module reports how the text fits.

For a poet writing in a stated form, who declares the meter they are aiming for and wants each line read against it.

- **Meter.** The poet picks a meter for the whole document from a list: iambic tetrameter, pentameter and hexameter; trochaic tetrameter, plain and catalectic (last foot short one syllable); anapestic trimeter and tetrameter; dactylic hexameter. Default: iambic pentameter. Any stanza can override it, or go back to "use the document default".
- **The choices belong to the poem.** They travel with the file, undo with edits, and show in every pane on the document. Turning the module off removes all marks and the rhyme column, leaves the text unchanged, and keeps the chosen meters for when it is turned on again. In a read-only pane everything is shown but the pickers change nothing.
- **Stanzas and lines.** A stanza is a run of consecutive non-blank paragraphs (one paragraph per line, the usual Enter-between-lines habit), ended by a blank paragraph. A paragraph that itself contains line breaks is a complete stanza on its own; each line break starts a new line. Other kinds of block, and text in notes inside the text, are not read as verse.
- **Scansion.** Under each line every syllable gets a mark for strong or weak, with a divider at each foot. Syllables whose natural stress breaks the declared meter are visibly marked, in the marks as well as in colour, and hovering a syllable explains the mark in plain words. The marks leave room in the text (taller lines, a wider left margin) without changing the document's content.
- **Best fit.** Scanning is the best reading, not a left-to-right zip: it tries the licensed liberties and keeps the reading with fewest violations. The liberties: elision (a small curated list such as every, ever, over, heaven, flower, power, prayer, spirit, evening, plus "-tion/-sion" squeezed to one beat); inversion of the first foot; a feminine ending (one extra weak syllable at the end); an added weak syllable in the middle (anapest); and a pyrrhic or spondee (two weak or two strong together). The report names the liberties used.
- **What has a fixed stress.** Only words of two or more syllables. One-syllable words, and words the pronouncing dictionary lacks, bend to the meter and are never flagged. If no reading fits even with the liberties, the line is reported as unscannable, with no invented marks or violations.
- **Rhyme scheme.** Detected from the poem's first non-blank stanza: lines whose end words rhyme perfectly (same sound from the last stressed vowel on) share a letter (ABAB and so on, in order of first appearance). If nothing rhymes there, no scheme is detected and none is enforced, and the report says why.
- **Rhyme display.** Each line shows its scheme letter in a left margin column, and where the pane is narrow, the pane scrolls sideways to it. Every line's end word is underlined, and hovering it names its slot and the words it should rhyme with. In later stanzas the scheme repeats if the stanza is longer and is cut if shorter. A line breaks the scheme, and turns red, when other lines in its stanza share its letter and none rhymes with it perfectly; a letter appearing once in a stanza always passes. Slant rhymes are not accepted.
- **The report.** A view beside the text shows the line the caret is in: whether it fits, how many spots break the meter, which liberties it used, and that stanza's meter picker. With the caret outside a line of verse it says to place the caret in a line. It follows the focused pane, and keeps clear of other modules' controls.
- **Dictionary.** Rhymes and stresses come from a pronouncing dictionary (CMU) shipped with the module: no network, no AI.
- **Cards.** When a pane shows the poem as cards, the same defaults and marks apply.
