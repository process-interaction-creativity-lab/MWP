# disappearing-sentences: help

You want to keep moving forward and stop rereading what you have already written. As you write, the sentences behind your caret fade to a soft grey, so only what you are working on stays clear.

## What it gives you

- **Fading** (the text itself, nothing to click): a sentence fades to a faint but legible grey about a second after your caret moves on to a later sentence.
- **Coming back** (the text itself): move the caret into a faded sentence, select text in it, or type in it, and it returns to full strength at once. Hovering does nothing.
- **Nothing to switch**: the module has no buttons or palette actions; turning it on is all it takes.

## Walkthrough

1. Turn disappearing-sentences on and start writing in an empty document.
2. Type three sentences, then begin a fourth. About a second after you start the second one, the first goes faint.
3. Keep typing. Each earlier sentence fades a second after you move past it, while the sentence you are in is never faint.
4. Click or arrow back into a faint sentence: it returns to full strength immediately. Sentences after the caret stay clear.
5. Select some faint text and copy it. It is still ordinary text and pastes intact.
6. Turn the module off to get everything at full strength; turn it on again and fading resumes.

## How the pieces fit

Fading is only a look: the text stays in your document, undo never involves it, and nothing about it is saved. Each pane fades by its own caret, and a pane you are not in is left as it is. Other panes and exports see the text normally.

## Tips

- Each paragraph starts a new sentence.
- Faded text can still be selected, found and edited.
- With reduced motion on in your system, the fade is immediate instead of smooth.
- The one-second delay is fixed; it is not a setting.
